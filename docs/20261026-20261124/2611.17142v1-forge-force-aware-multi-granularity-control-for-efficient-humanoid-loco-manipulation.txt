I. INTRODUCTION
Humanoid loco-manipulation requires coordinating ma-
nipulation with stepping, leaning, or posture adjustment un-
der complex dynamics and frequent contacts [1]–[3]. Unlike
fixed-base dual-arm robots, which benefit from stable support
and mature teleoperation systems [4], humanoids must map
operator intent to whole-body behavior under underactua-
tion and changing contacts. Balance errors can compromise
stability, joint errors can accumulate into end-effector drift,
and rigid position control can disrupt sustained interaction. A
practical controller must therefore jointly provide Stability,
arXiv:2611.17142v1  [cs.RO]  12 Nov 2026


Precision, and Contact Awareness, including interaction-
force estimation for regulating sustained contact.
Existing approaches satisfy these requirements only in
part. Full-body tracking and large-scale motor-control mod-
els [5]–[7] provide broad motion repertoires, but dense
motion-reference objectives do not directly expose task-space
commands for precise foot placement, wrist targeting, and
sustained contact. Decoupled locomotion/upper-body stacks
[8], [9] preserve locomotion stability but can limit task-
dependent whole-body coordination. Force-aware approaches
[10]–[12] demonstrate learned force estimation and force-
conditioned position control, yet sensorless estimation is
difficult: external contact, friction, actuator behavior, and
whole-body coupling are entangled in proprioception.
A unified controller must therefore support coarse-to-fine
teleoperation: approaching a task region, adjusting stance,
tracking wrist trajectories, and adapting to contact. Com-
mands must remain composable as task demands change.
These limitations motivate FORGE, which forges a
FORce-aware multi-Granularity policy for Efficient hu-
manoid loco-manipulation. Its unified coarse-to-fine interface
supports base velocity commands, planar foot-placement
targets, Cartesian wrist-pose targets, and optional force-
conditioning inputs, individually or jointly. Granularity-
conditioned masking [13] activates the observations and
rewards relevant to each command group. Training needs no
full-body human trajectories or human joint-angle references;
teleoperation supplies only bilateral wrist poses relative to
the pelvis and coarse commands from wearable trackers,
handheld VR devices, or vision-based pose estimation.
For sustained contact, a Physics-Informed Hybrid Force
Estimator learns a simulator-supervised correction to a
model-based momentum observer. At deployment, it uses
arm joint states and reconstructed low-level PD torques
without wrist force/torque sensors. The estimator captures
the low-frequency component of the equivalent interaction
force at each wrist. We combine this estimate with an
optional command for the robot-applied force to adjust wrist
position targets during sustained, quasi-static contact.
To stabilize mixed-objective training, we freeze a pre-
trained lower-body locomotion expert and learn an asymmet-
ric residual policy that controls the upper body and applies
corrective lower-body actions. This reduces exploration dif-
ficulty while preserving the locomotion prior and allowing
task-dependent whole-body coordination.
Simulation and hardware experiments show that masking
and asymmetric residual training improve unified-control
stability and task-space tracking over their ablations, while
the hybrid estimator reduces force-estimation error under the
evaluated quasi-static contacts compared with analytical-only
and learned-only estimators. To assess FORGE as a whole-
body executor for visuomotor learning, we train a diffu-
sion policy [14] on collected demonstrations; it commands
FORGE to execute squat-and-lift across payloads.
Our contributions are a unified multi-granularity controller
trained without full-body human motion references, physics-
informed sensorless wrist-force estimation with contact-
load adaptation, and asymmetric residual learning for stable
whole-body loco-manipulation.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Whole-Body Control and Teleoperation Systems
Humanoid loco-manipulation controllers broadly follow
two paradigms. Motion-reference-driven policies learn broad
whole-body behaviors from retargeted or captured motions
[5]–[7], [15], [16], whereas modular systems separate learned
locomotion from upper-body control [8], [9]. The former
relies on dense whole-body references; the latter preserves
locomotion stability but can restrict task-dependent whole-
body coordination. SoFTA explicitly separates slow loco-
motion and fast upper-body control to stabilize the end
effectors during walking [17]. Related work also explores
masked motion generation and reusable behavior priors [13],
[18]. In contrast, FORGE trains from task-space commands
without full-body human motion references and uses a frozen
locomotion prior with asymmetric residual learning [19],
[20]. Its teleoperation input comprises only pelvis-relative
wrist poses and coarse commands, whether obtained from
wearable, handheld, or vision-based trackers.
Building on 3D Diffusion Policy [21], Improved 3D Dif-
fusion Policy combines upper-body teleoperation with 3D
visuomotor learning on a cart-supported humanoid [22]. Our
downstream evaluation instead uses a diffusion policy to
command a balancing whole-body executor.
B. Force Perception and Compliant Interaction
Humanoid controllers increasingly incorporate interaction-
aware or force–position objectives [23], [24], while sensor-
less estimators infer wrist interaction from proprioception
[10]–[12]. UniFP estimates external force from propriocep-
tive history and maps force–position commands to compen-
sated position targets [10]. FORGE retains this separation
but adds a momentum-observer prior before learned correc-
tion. It estimates low-frequency translational wrist interac-
tion for quasi-static virtual-target adaptation, not full-wrench
sensing or dynamic impedance control.
III. METHOD
FORGE combines a compact multi-granularity command
interface with a force-aware contact pathway. Navigation,
stance, and wrist-pose commands share one whole-body
policy; a Physics-Informed Hybrid Force Estimator and
virtual-target loop provide sustained-contact adaptation with-
out wrist force/torque sensors. Asymmetric Residual Ac-
tions anchor this policy on a frozen locomotion prior (Fig. 2).
A. Multi-Granularity Control System
One policy accepts four individually or jointly active
command groups: base velocity vcmd (Navigation), planar
left/right footholds pfeet ∈R2×2 (Stance), bilateral wrist
poses Twrist ∈SE(3)2 (Pose), and optional desired robot-
applied wrist forces fcmd ∈R2×3 (Force). An operator or
upstream model may supply the force command for virtual-
target updates (Sec. III-B), not direct torque control.

C. Asymmetric Residual Whole-Body Control
Mixed locomotion, pose, and contact objectives can desta-
bilize gait learning because unrestricted whole-body explo-
ration can sacrifice support behavior to reduce short-horizon
manipulation errors. We therefore train a lower-body expert
with upper-body actions fixed to zero, freeze it, and learn
a whole-body residual ∆a = [∆aℓ; ∆au] ∈R29. Freezing
the expert prevents gradients from manipulation and contact
objectives from rewriting the nominal gait, while the train-
able lower-body residual still permits command-dependent
foothold and balance corrections. The residual policy re-
ceives the active multi-granularity command and learns these
bounded leg corrections alongside direct upper-body actions.
Summing the frozen action and residual before executing
one whole-body action ensures that stance adjustment and
manipulation-induced posture changes remain coupled rather
than handled by independent controllers. With 15 lower-body
and 14 upper-body dimensions, the executed action is

IV. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION
We evaluate four aspects of FORGE: (i) how its hard-
ware latency and task-space tracking compare with existing
humanoid controllers; (ii) whether one policy supports multi-
ple command granularities without destabilizing locomotion;
(iii) whether the hybrid estimator improves wrist-equivalent
interaction-force estimation and supports contact-load adap-
tation; and (iv) whether FORGE serves as a downstream
whole-body executor for visuomotor manipulation. Fig. 5
shows sequential bottle placement and the estimated vertical
wrist-force responses around bottle release.
A. Implementation Details
Unless otherwise specified, ablation variants share the
training settings and environment randomization, except for
the components being ablated. Early training fixes yaw-rate
commands at zero, smooths torque commands with EMA
(α = 0.3), and applies left–right symmetric augmentation.
Task-space termination is disabled for the first 300 steps of
every episode and until 2,000 global updates; afterward, we
use δterm = 0.2 m. The lower-body residual action standard
deviation is clipped at 0.2.

four minibatches, and gradient-norm limit 1.0. Both stages
are capped at 20,000 updates. Isaac Gym advances at 200 Hz

Contact-sensitive teleoperation: We lift the same foam box
with or without force feedback (Fig. 9a), using identical
policy weights, initial pose, nominal wrist trajectory, and
lift target. Disabling feedback tracks nominal wrist targets
directly; enabling it adapts them using estimated interaction
forces. Both complete the lift, but feedback (left) produces
less visible deformation than the baseline (right).
Force estimation accuracy: We evaluate force estimation on
G1 hardware using manually applied wrist loads measured
with a handheld force gauge (Fig. 9b). The gauge is aligned
with each evaluated axis, and its readings provide a digi-

uses arm joint states and reconstructed PD torques without
wrist force/torque sensors. Throughout estimator evaluation,
fcmd = 0 and every gauge load is externally applied. We
repeat each load condition 10 times for staged 0–20 N loads
on all axes, signed planar forces in [−30, +30] N, and held-
out vertical loads of 35/40/50 N. Fig. 8 shows representative
trials; shading in Fig. 9c shows one standard deviation
over ten trials. The learned-only baseline follows UniFP’s
proprioceptive-history estimator design [10].

F. Demonstration Collection and Downstream Learning
Diffusion Policy as a validation: We use Diffusion Policy
as an upstream visuomotor planner and FORGE as the only
whole-body joint-action executor. One cross-payload model
maps head-mounted RGB and task state to multi-granularity
command chunks. Training pools 80 demonstrations from
each of four payload conditions (320 total).
We evaluate this pipeline over 20 hardware trials for each
payload on the squat-and-lift-box transport task with empty,
0.5 kg, 1 kg, and 2 kg boxes. A trial succeeds only when the
robot completes the prescribed transport sequence without
task termination or human intervention. As shown in Table V,
the complete pipeline succeeds in 20/20, 20/20, 18/20, and
18/20 trials, corresponding to success rates of 100%, 100%,
90%, and 90%, respectively. Assuming independent trials,
the 95% Wilson intervals are 83.9–100% for 20/20 successes
and 69.9–97.2% for 18/20 successes. The same Diffusion
Policy–FORGE pipeline completes the transport task across
the tested 0–2 kg payload range.
V. CONCLUSION
FORGE combines multi-granularity commands, sensor-
less force estimation, and asymmetric residual whole-body
control. Hardware experiments demonstrate task-space track-
ing, contact-aware manipulation, and execution of Diffusion
Policy commands across tested payloads.
