## 摘要
使人形机器人能够与人类伙伴自然交互，不仅需要保留个体动作，还需要保留交互背后所蕴含的空间、语义和接触关系（例如拥抱、跳舞、握手等）。此外，一个实用的解决方案应能泛化到具有不同形态和运动学约束的各种人形机器人具身。为应对这一挑战，我们提出 HumiTransfer，一个模仿式具身迁移框架，可将真实场景中的人-人交互视频转换为物理上合理的人-人形机器人交互。HumiTransfer 首先重建具有一致足-地接触的 3D 人体运动，然后通过交互建模将其中一名参与者迁移到目标人形机器人具身，使用加权关节相对方向和解剖学偏移来优先保持对交互至关重要的身体间关系，同时通过重定向后优化来施加平衡、接触和碰撞避免约束。随后，我们将 HumiTransfer 应用于 1,200 段真实场景视频，为两款人形机器人生成约 500,000 帧的多样化交互数据，从而构建了我们的 HumiMimic 数据集。在两款机器人上，与现有基线相比，HumiTransfer 均实现了更优的运动保真度，保持了身体间关系，并维持了接触准确性。此外，我们证明 HumiMimic 可支持运动生成方法，用于合成跟随人类伙伴的交互式人形机器人运动。最后，我们的真实机器人部署验证了迁移运动在真实环境中的物理可执行性。项目地址：https://aioznetwork.github.io/HumiMimic/

## Abstract
Enabling a humanoid to interact naturally with human partners requires preserving not only individual motions but also the spatial, semantic, and contact relationships underlying the interaction (i.e., hugging, dancing, hand-shaking, etc.). Furthermore, a practical solution should generalize across various humanoid embodiments with distinct morphologies and kinematic constraints. To tackle this challenge, we propose HumiTransfer, a mimetic embodiment transfer framework that converts in-the-wild human-human interaction videos into physically plausible human-humanoid interactions. HumiTransfer first reconstructs 3D human motion with consistent foot-ground contact, then transfers one participant to a target humanoid embodiment via interaction modeling, using weighted joint-relative directions and anatomical offsets to prioritize interaction-critical inter-body relationships, while post-retargeting optimization enforces balance, contact, and collision avoidance. Subsequently, we apply HumiTransfer to 1,200 in-the-wild videos, producing approximately 500,000 frames of diverse interactions for two humanoid robots, resulting in our HumiMimic dataset. Across both robots, HumiTransfer achieves superior motion fidelity, preserves inter-body relationships, and maintains contact accuracy compared with existing baselines. Moreover, we demonstrate that HumiMimic supports motion generation methods for synthesizing interactive humanoid motions following a human partner. Finally, our real robot deployments confirm the physical executability of the transferred motions in real-world settings. Our project is available at https://aioznetwork.github.io/HumiMimic/
