PERA: Plug-in Experts for Robotic Actions
in Continual Whole-Body Control
Shangchao Hu
Yadan Sun
Xibo Yu
Wei Huang
Abstract
Motion imitation enables humanoid robots to acquire whole-body skills such as walking
and dancing. However, fine-tuning an entire policy for a new skill can affect existing behaviors,
while training a separate policy for each skill increases training and maintenance costs. We
present PERA, a method for learning new skills by reusing existing motion capabilities.
PERA freezes a pretrained mixture-of-experts policy and trains an additional task expert and
its routing score using task rewards or teacher-action supervision. In Unitree G1 simulation,
each added task module contains approximately 1.03% of the parameters of the base actor.
Built on our pretrained policy, PERA successfully completes all ten new tasks. Disabling the
expert restores the original computation path of the base policy, which still fully executes
all 126 original motions. Enabling the expert also improves the execution of some dance
motions not used to train that expert, indicating that its benefits extend beyond its training
motions. Experiments with HoloMotion further demonstrate the applicability of PERA to
different base policies.
Keywords: humanoid robots; whole-body control; mixture of experts; skill expansion; online
DAgger

Introduction
Reference-based motion imitation provides an effective approach to humanoid whole-body control.
With advances in motion data and policy learning, a single controller can perform walking,
dancing, and a range of highly dynamic motions [1, 2, 3]. However, new tasks may involve
contact patterns, posture constraints, or motion objectives not covered during training. Existing
motion-tracking capabilities alone do not guarantee success on these tasks. It therefore remains
necessary to learn new skills while preserving access to existing motion capabilities.
Directly fine-tuning an entire policy applies new-task gradients to shared parameters and can
change existing control behaviors. Maintaining a separate controller for each new task can
isolate parameter updates, but requires repeated model storage and makes it difficult to share
computation during execution. Skill expansion therefore calls for both reuse of existing motion
knowledge and independent access to the original policy. We address this objective by combining
a frozen base policy with additional task modules (Figure 1).
Mixture-of-experts (MoE) networks provide a structural basis for incremental expansion. Experts
reuse shared features and an action-output interface, while routing determines their participation
and mixing weights from the input. Task-related feature transformations can thus be learned
within an additional module. A new candidate can also change the selection and mixing of
existing experts, so control outputs may change even when shared parameters remain fixed.
Expert expansion must therefore specify both how additional modules are learned and how the
original policy path is recovered when they are disabled.

Our main contributions are: (1) an expert-expansion architecture and training method for
continual robot skill learning, in which new experts reuse existing motion representations while
the pretrained policy remains frozen; (2) an evaluation of the improvements provided by expert
learning, showing higher task completion and improved tracking accuracy on some unseen
motions; and (3) a modular skill library that supports independent training, storage, and
on-demand invocation of task experts, allowing the skill set to grow through incremental module
additions.

Related Work
Whole-body motion control.
DeepMimic [1] combines reference-motion imitation with task
rewards to learn dynamic skills such as walking, rolling, and martial arts through reinforcement
learning. PHC [2] progressively increases policy capacity to learn large motion collections and
incorporates fall recovery, enabling continued execution under noisy references and unexpected
loss of balance. SONIC [3] scales motion tracking along model capacity, motion data, and
compute, and connects teleoperation and motion-planning interfaces through a unified motion
representation, demonstrating the role of large-scale motion learning in general whole-body
2


control. KungfuBot2 [4] uses an orthogonal mixture-of-experts architecture to promote distinct
motion representations, together with hybrid local–global tracking objectives and a segment-level
reward to improve multi-skill and long-horizon motion control. This work studies versatile
skills within a unified policy; PERA focuses on adding independently learned task experts after
freezing a pretrained policy.
Continual skill learning.
Similarity-guided LoRA-PNN [5] selects reusable historical skills
based on motion similarity and combines low-rank adaptation with progressive network ex-
pansion to learn new motions, retaining historical branches while facilitating transfer across
skills. LiMoDE [6] addresses lifelong robot manipulation by first learning dynamic experts
through multitask pretraining and then combining new and frozen experts for new tasks. DM-
PEL [7] progressively constructs a low-rank expert library, dynamically combines experts with a
lightweight router, and uses expert coefficient replay to preserve expert invocation on historical
tasks, balancing adaptation and knowledge retention.
Expert modules and knowledge integration.
MoE-Loco [8] uses expert specialization
to alleviate gradient conflicts in multitask reinforcement learning, enabling a single policy to
handle multiple terrains and gaits while exhibiting specialized expert behaviors. FlexOlmo [9]
addresses language-model learning when training data cannot be shared: data owners train
experts independently, then combine them through domain-informed routing, allowing modules
to be included or excluded at inference time. CPC [10] separates skill acquisition from policy
consolidation by training task teachers independently through reinforcement learning, continually
distilling their behaviors into a unified student, and mitigating forgetting with an expandable
expert architecture and prioritized trajectory replay.
Pretrained control policies and model-assisted learning.
HoloMotion-1 [11] trains a
sparse MoE Transformer on video-reconstructed motions, motion-capture data, and other motion
sources. Temporal modeling, sparse expert activation, and sequence-level training improve
whole-body tracking, with evaluations of unseen-motion generalization and real-robot transfer.
SuperMimic [12] jointly learns a differentiable world model and a control policy, optimizing the
policy by backpropagating through short predicted trajectories to reduce the training cost of
large-scale motion tracking.
3
Method
PERA first pretrains a base policy on reference motions, then freezes its parameters and statistics
and adds a feedforward network (FFN) expert and a scoring branch for each new task. The new
and existing experts share the action-output head. Task rewards or teacher supervision update
only the additional parameters. Figure 2 illustrates learning and execution.
3.1
Pretrained Whole-Body Motion-Tracking Policy
The base policy follows the reference-motion encoder and observation-history decoder structure
of SuperMimic [12]. The encoder extracts target features from a normalized reference-motion

Policy pretraining alternates simulator sampling, world-model fitting, and policy updates. A
differentiable world model learns dynamics from collected state transitions, and the actor is
updated using gradients of predicted returns over 32 steps. After pretraining, the existing
experts, router, encoder, output head, and normalization statistics are frozen.

3.2
Task-Expert Learning
The FFN of an additional task expert is initialized by copying parameters from an existing
expert, and then learns the new task while the base policy remains frozen.
Training updates only the additional FFN and scoring branch; the base-policy parameters and
normalization statistics remain frozen. Losses are computed from the actions of the expanded
policy, and gradients propagate through the frozen output head to the additional module.
Experts receive the normalized decoder feature ¯xt. The router uses the frozen routing-feature
embedding ht; task context ct is also supplied to the new expert and its scoring branch when
required.
The router assigns each candidate expert a raw score ℓi. Adding a fixed bias δi gives the selection
score vi:
vi = ℓi + δi,

The existing experts retain their pretrained biases, and the new expert’s bias is determined
before task training. All selection biases remain fixed during task training and testing.

Reward-based learning.
When a task has an explicit reward function, the expanded policy
collects state, action, and reward sequences in simulation. PPO [13] updates the additional FFN
and scoring branch using these samples. The value network is trained separately, and the base
policy remains frozen.
Teacher supervision.
When a reliable teacher is available, behavior cloning (BC) first trains
the additional module to match the teacher’s actions. Online DAgger [14] then uses the teacher
to label actions at states visited by the policy; the new samples are added to the training set for
continued learning. The probability of executing teacher actions is gradually reduced until the
expanded policy executes independently.
3.3
Expert Invocation and Original-Policy Recovery
Each task expert independently stores its additional FFN, scoring branch, and fixed selection bias.
During execution, the corresponding module can be enabled to participate in Top-2 selection.
Disabling it bypasses the module and restores the original routing and action computation of
the base policy. Because the base-policy parameters and normalization statistics remain frozen,
disabling the module produces the same action as the base policy for the same input.
4
Experiments
We first evaluate the original motion capabilities after disabling the added expert and transfer
to unseen motions when the expert is enabled. We then compare our pretrained policy and
HoloMotion-1 [11] after adding PERA experts on ten scenarios, using task teachers as references.
4.1
Evaluation Setup and Base Policies
Experiments use a simulated 29-degree-of-freedom Unitree G1. Our pretrained policy implements
an MoE network using multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), with eight routed experts and one shared
expert. The actor contains approximately 10.4 million parameters.
The ten new tasks use reference motions and paired terrain assets released with Deep Whole-body
Parkour [15]. They comprise dive rolling, kneel climbing, jump sitting, and roll vaulting, forming
ten motion–scene combinations across different terrains and wall settings (S01–S10; Table 1).
All policies use the same reference motion and terrain for each scenario. The control frequency is
50 Hz, and episodes begin at the first reference frame with the native failure conditions retained.

Expert Expansion on Different Base Policies
To evaluate PERA on different base policies, we add task experts to our pretrained policy and
to HoloMotion, then compare endpoint and root position errors on the same ten tasks, with
task teachers as references.

errors are lower than the teacher’s on four tasks and higher on the remaining task (Figure 5).

5
Conclusion
We introduced PERA, which expands robot skills by adding task experts to a frozen pretrained
motion policy. Each additional expert and its scoring branch are trained independently and
participate in action generation alongside existing experts. Experts can be enabled on demand
during execution; disabling them restores the original computation path of the base policy,
supporting both new-task learning and access to the original policy.
In Unitree G1 simulation, PERA built on our pretrained policy successfully completes all ten new
tasks, while disabling the expert preserves full execution of the 126 original motions. Enabling an
expert also improves some dance motions not used to train it, showing that its effects can extend
beyond its training motions. Experiments with HoloMotion further demonstrate applicability to
different base policies. These results support reusing existing motion capabilities and expanding
robot skills through independently trained task experts that can be plugged in on demand.
